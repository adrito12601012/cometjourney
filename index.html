The Journey of Comet

Comet's Identity

Coronal Mass Ejections (CME) can cause beautiful aurorae, however can be problematic for electrical systems, satellites, radio and GPS systems. CMEs can be extremely hazardous for astronauts and cause them to receive a large dose of radiation due to them having less protection. Pilots and passengers in aircraft at very high altitudes can also receive small doses. In space, they can cause problems for electronic systems in satellites and disrupt the magnetic field of a planet. However, scientists find them very useful. As they occur, we can learn new information about the relationship between Earth and the Sun, understand space weather and discover just how active our solar system is!

The Naughty Fire Ball

Comet is a cheeky little fireball zooming through space, leaving glittering tails behind! It plays hide-and-seek with the Sun, sometimes sneaking close to show off its glowing tail. Packed with icy secrets from the start of the Solar System, Comet loves surprising everyone with its wild, twirling dance across the sky.

Quick Science

Just like every comet has its own sparkling identity with a glowing tail, the Sun has its own ‘naughty fireball bursts’ called CMEs (Coronal Mass Ejection) — huge waves of energy that zoom across space! While comets show us their tails for fun, CMEs send out invisible UV light and solar radiation, reminding us that even the Sun has a wild personality.

  • Solar Blast: A CME is like a huge bubble of hot gas and magnetic energy shooting out from the Sun.
  • Magnetic Magic: When CMEs hit Earth, they can create auroras—colorful lights in the sky near the poles!
  • Skin Alert: Too much solar radiation can sunburn or harm our skin cells.
  • Invisible Rays: It carries UV light and solar radiation, which we can’t see but can feel.
  • Stay Safe: Scientists track CMEs so people and gadgets can be protected from strong solar storms.
Above the clouds

Aurora

The aurora occurs high in the atmosphere, near the poles. Occasionally, the Sun will release small charged particles. These travel to Earth, and collide with gases in the atmosphere causing light. This creates beautiful green, pink and purple waves in the sky. Sometimes the aurora looks like a curtain, and sometimes like a dragon. Although very beautiful, this is caused by a very powerful and slightly dangerous source, the Sun!

Aurora’s Sky Dance

High up in the sky, the Aurora dances like a colorful fairy! When the Sun sends sparkly particles to Earth, they bump into our air and paint the sky with green, pink, and purple lights.

Quick Science

High above the clouds, near the poles, the sky sometimes throws a color party! These glowing waves are called Auroras — when the Sun’s charged particles meet Earth’s atmosphere, they paint the sky with magical lights of green, pink, and purple. It’s science that looks like magic!

  • Solar Connection: Auroras happen when charged particles from the Sun (from a CME or solar wind) enter Earth’s atmosphere.
  • Sky Glow Zone: This light show happens in the ionosphere, a layer full of electrically charged gases.
  • Satellite Trouble: Strong solar activity can disturb satellite signals, making GPS or radio systems glitch.
  • Pilot & Human Effects: Pilots may see auroras from the cockpit, but high doses of radiation during strong solar storms can be harmful — so scientists keep watch to stay safe!

The Earthlings

People working in different fields view the deeds of Comet in different ways. Starting from astronauts and pilots to general public, the impacts are present everywhere. Let's have a look to what those earthlings think, shall we?

Astronauts

Astronauts are working in spacecrafts very high up in the atmosphere. In these spacecrafts, they perform many tasks such as repairing equipment, carrying out experiments and eating floating bubble-like food! Occasionally, solar activity (Solar storms and CMEs) can cause problems. The large amounts of energy cause disruption to signals in space. The GPS, communication and even WiFi may become unreliable. Because of this, astronauts and scientists are always very aware of what is happening with the Sun. Although working in space is very exciting, it is important to be very aware of solar storms.

Astronauts and the Sun’s Space Pranks!

Astronauts do have a lot of fun in the outer space... They can navigate planets, stars and so much interesting and funny things. They always be in touch with it. But sometimes the sun plays tricks with them. The suns burst solar energy interrupts astronauts GPS movements and confuse them about their paths. But intelligent astronauts find their paths in sneaky ways!

Quick Science

High above Earth, the sky sometimes lights up with dancing colors — green, pink, and purple! These are Auroras, sparkling ribbons caused by the Sun’s particles colliding with our atmosphere. It’s like a magical space party, but it’s also real science that can affect astronauts and technology.

  • Color Chemistry: Oxygen makes green lights, while nitrogen makes pink and purple ones.
  • Pilot & Astronaut Alert: Astronauts and high-flying pilots must be careful because solar radiation can be higher near auroras.
  • Protective Measures: Scientists monitor space weather and auroras to keep astronauts, satellites, and communications safe.
  • Solar Sparks: Auroras happen when charged particles from the Sun crash into Earth’s atmosphere.

Pilots

Pilots fly large aircrafts very high in the atmosphere to transport people between cities. They rely on GPS and equipment in order to accurately navigate. If there is solar flare activity or a space storm, the GPS may become unreliable and therefore difficult to navigate. In this situation, pilots will use back-up equipment, maps and navigation by sight. Occasionally, during their flight, they may even be able to see beautiful aurorae! Despite the Sun acting badly, pilots are able to safely return everyone to where they need to be.

Leo’s Flight Through Dancing Lights

High in the fluffy clouds, pilots are brave sky heroes steering big airplanes like metal birds! Sometimes the Sun sends sneaky sparkles that make GPS and radios act silly, but pilots stay calm, use backup tools, and guide everyone safely while the sky glows with dancing rainbow lights.

Quick Science

High above the clouds, the Sun isn’t just bright — it sometimes sends invisible energy bursts that can shake the sky! These solar bursts, called solar flares and geomagnetic storms, can tickle instruments, wiggle signals, and sprinkle extra radiation on pilots and passengers. Science helps us stay safe while flying through these space surprises!

  • Solar Flares: Bursts of light and energy from the Sun can disturb GPS and radio signals used in airplanes.
  • Geomagnetic Storms: When Earth’s magnetic field wobbles, instruments like compasses and autopilot can act funny or confused.
  • High-Altitude Radiation: Pilots and passengers at high altitudes get a tiny extra dose of solar radiation, like invisible sparkles from the Sun.
  • Aurora Effects: Flying near the poles, colorful auroras show Sun activity and can slightly interfere with instruments.

Farmers

Farmers grow the food that we eat. They plant seeds, water the crops and constantly care for their fields. Many farmers now use GPS guided tractors and drones in order to speed up their process. However, if there is a solar flare or space storm, the GPS signal can be unstable. This means that tractors could go off course, drones may spin or get lost. The farmer must use a back up system or wait until the Sun has settled. However, regardless of the weather in the sky, farmers will continue to care for their crops. They may just have to change their method.

The Dancing Tractors and Dizzy Drones

Out in fields big and small, farmers are the earth superheroes who plant seeds, water crops, and watch plants grow tall! Some have tricky tractors and buzzing drones to help them work faster, but sometimes the Sun sends wiggly space storms that make GPS and machines go silly. Even then, farmers stay clever, use backup tricks, and keep our food growing strong!

Quick Science

The Sun isn’t just bright and warm — sometimes it sends bursts of energy called solar flares and geomagnetic storms that reach Earth! These invisible wiggles in the sky can confuse GPS and satellites, making tractors and drones go a little dizzy. But farmers use clever tricks and backups to keep their fields growing strong, turning science into farm magic!

  • Geomagnetic Storms: When Earth’s magnetic field shakes, GPS signals can wobble, making machines follow crooked paths.
  • Drone Disruptions: Flying drones can spin or lose their way when space weather scrambles their satellite signals.
  • Tractor Challenges: GPS-guided tractors may draw squiggly lines instead of straight rows.
  • Safety Measures: Farmers can wait for calm conditions or use manual tools and ropes to guide crops safely.

Power Grid Operators

Operators manage the power grid. The power grid is a large system of wires, transformers and machinery. The electricity generated from solar panels, wind farms and power stations is transported through this grid to homes, schools and hospitals. From control rooms filled with screens and graphs, operators manage the grid. Solar flares and CMEs can cause problems in the power grid. The energy from millions of miles away can cause excess current to move through power lines. Transformers can become very hot and solar panels can increase rapidly in output. In order to manage this, operators have back-up circuits, clever switches and cooling systems. At times, they may also transfer the electricity to a different route. Through careful planning and teamwork, the lights stay on despite the Sun acting badly!

Quick Science

Power grid operators are like electricity superheroes, keeping power flowing from solar panels, wind farms, and power plants to homes and schools. Sometimes the Sun sends bursts of energy that make lines wiggle and transformers heat up. Operators use backup circuits and smart switches to keep electricity safe, showing how teamwork and planning beat the Sun’s tricky tricks.

  • Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs): Huge eruptions from the Sun’s surface can induce currents in Earth’s wires, making transformers heat up.
  • Geomagnetic Storms: Changes in Earth’s magnetic field from the Sun can make power grids act wobbly or surge.
  • Transformer Warnings: Transformers can overheat or trip if extra energy sneaks in through power lines.
  • Solar Panel Surges: Panels that collect Sun energy can spike unexpectedly, so backup systems are needed.

General Public

Ultraviolet (UV) light from the Sun is invisible. Riley loves running outside as she enjoys feeling the sun on her nose. Sunlight allows flowers to grow and helps us produce vitamin D in our bodies so we can have strong bones. However, if we are exposed to too much sunlight it can cause our skin to become pink and sore, cause damage to our eyes and cause toys to become hot. As a result, it is important to wear hats, sunglasses, sun cream and to be in some shade. However, by forming simple habits, we can enjoy the sun safely and gain the positive benefits!

Riley and the Sun’s Bouncy Rays!

The Sun gives us light, warmth, and energy, but it also sends UV rays that can pinch skin, heat toys, or sneak into eyes. Wearing hats, sunglasses, and sunscreen, and taking breaks in the shade, helps everyone enjoy sunlight safely while still growing strong and happy.

Quick Science

The Sun gives us light, warmth, and invisible rays called UV that help our bodies grow strong and make vitamin D. But too much UV can burn skin, hurt eyes, or heat up toys and gadgets. Quick science teaches us how to enjoy the Sun safely with hats, sunscreen, and shade.

  • UV Rays: Invisible Sun rays carrying extra energy that can burn skin if too strong.
  • Vitamin D: Sunlight helps bodies make vitamin D for strong bones.
  • Skin Protection: Sunscreen acts like a magic shield, blocking UV pinches.
  • Eye Safety: Sunglasses protect eyes from UV rays like superhero visors.
  • Toy Protection: Sunlight can heat up toys or gadgets, so shade helps.
  • Short Sun Play: A little sunlight is good and fun, but too long can sting.
  • Fun Safety Kit: Hats, shades, and sunscreen let kids play safely while enjoying Sun energy.
